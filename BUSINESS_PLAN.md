# Website Development Side Business — First Draft

> Draft version: 1.0  
> Market: Malaysia  
> Currency: Malaysian Ringgit (MYR)

## 1. Business idea

Help SMEs, small businesses, freelancers, local shops, individuals, and early-stage startups build a professional online presence without the cost or complexity of a large agency. This includes business websites, personal websites, portfolios, service websites, and campaign pages.

The business will offer three clear service levels:

1. **Basic Website** — a fast, polished website for presenting a business, service, portfolio, or campaign.
2. **Editable Website** — a website connected to a simple admin dashboard so the client can manage products or content.
3. **Custom Build** — tailored websites, internal tools, portals, integrations, and full web applications priced after requirement discovery.

## 2. Positioning

### Simple promise

> Professional websites for Malaysian businesses, delivered clearly, affordably, and without unnecessary complexity.

### Differentiators

- Direct access to a software engineer.
- Clear fixed-scope packages instead of vague hourly estimates.
- Mobile-first, fast-loading, and SEO-ready implementation.
- Practical handover and training for clients who want to manage their own website.
- Ability to grow from a basic website into a custom system when the business needs more.

### Technology direction

- **Frontend:** React for reusable, responsive, and maintainable website interfaces.
- **Database:** PostgreSQL for products, services, users, and other structured business data.
- **Basic Website:** React-based frontend deployed to the client’s hosting platform.
- **Editable Website:** React frontend connected to a backend and PostgreSQL database, with an admin portal for content management.
- **Custom Build:** React and PostgreSQL where appropriate, with additional services or integrations selected according to the project requirements.

### Package 3 — Custom Build

**Custom quote only — no fixed public price**

This package is for requirements that do not fit the first two packages. It can include:

- Custom business applications.
- Booking and appointment systems.
- Customer or member portals.
- E-commerce and payment workflows.
- Dashboards and reporting.
- Internal tools and approval workflows.
- API and third-party integrations.
- Automation and data migration.
- Multi-role platforms.
- Mobile-friendly web applications that work well on phones, tablets, and desktop browsers.
- Progressive web apps or other web-based applications.

#### Custom project process

1. **Discovery call** — understand the business, users, and desired outcome.
2. **Requirements summary** — document features, assumptions, risks, and exclusions.
3. **Proposal and estimate** — provide milestones, timeline, price, and payment schedule.
4. **Prototype or technical plan** — agree on the workflow before full development.
5. **Build and review** — deliver in milestones with regular demonstrations.
6. **Testing and launch** — verify the agreed acceptance criteria.
7. **Handover and support** — provide documentation and training. Future updates are quoted separately when requested.

The initial discovery phase is **free**. This includes an introductory discussion, understanding the business problem, identifying the likely solution, and recommending the next step. A formal quotation is provided after the initial discovery discussion.

## 4. Third-party costs and one-time setup services

Keep one-time development fees separate from third-party costs and setup services. The client should normally pay third-party providers directly.

- Domain registration and renewal.
- Hosting or cloud infrastructure. Hosting setup assistance can be provided as a separate paid service.
- Transaction/payment-provider fees.
- Email services.
- Premium fonts, plugins, themes, APIs, or stock assets.
- Future maintenance, security updates, backups, and content changes are handled as separate one-off requests when needed.

### Future support and updates

We do not offer recurring maintenance or hosting plans as part of the standard packages. If the client needs changes after launch, each request is reviewed and quoted separately before work begins.

Suggested one-off support rate: **RM150/hour, one-hour minimum**, or a fixed price for a clearly defined update.

### Hosting setup assistance

Hosting is not included in the one-time development price unless stated in the quotation. Clients may either:

1. Provide their own hosting account, with setup assistance charged separately where applicable; or
2. Ask us to recommend a suitable provider and help them set up, configure, and deploy the website for an additional fee.

The client should own the hosting account and pay the hosting provider directly. We can help with provider selection, account setup, domain connection, DNS, SSL, deployment, email configuration, and basic backup setup. Ongoing hosting management or server administration is not included in the standard service.

Hosting-related charges should be listed separately on every quotation. The quote should identify the provider, plan, billing period, renewal price, included resources, and which setup tasks are included. The client pays any recurring provider fees directly; these costs should never be hidden inside the development fee.

Suggested hosting setup charges:

| Service | Suggested price |
|---|---:|
| Hosting provider recommendation and account setup guidance | RM150–RM300 one-time |
| Domain, DNS, SSL, and website deployment setup | RM300–RM800 one-time |
| Hosting migration | From RM500 |
| Ongoing server/hosting management | Quote separately |

## 5. Payment and project terms

### Recommended payment schedule

- **50% deposit** before work starts.
- **30% milestone payment** after design approval or the first working build.
- **20% final payment** before production launch or transfer of final assets.

For small projects below RM3,000, use **50% deposit and 50% before launch**.

### Important terms to include in every proposal

- Exact number of pages, features, and revision rounds.
- What content the client must provide and by what date.
- Project timeline starts after the deposit and receipt of required content.
- Delayed client feedback moves the delivery date.
- New features are handled through a change request and may change price/timeline.
- Final source code, credentials, and transfer happen after final payment.
- A limited post-launch defect warranty, such as 14–30 days.
- Hosting, domain, and third-party fees are not included unless stated.
- The client owns the final project deliverables after payment; reusable code, tools, and templates remain the developer’s property unless otherwise agreed.
- Permission to display the completed project in a portfolio, unless the client requests confidentiality.

## 6. Sales and delivery workflow

### Lead qualification

Ask every prospect:

- What does the business do?
- Who is the target customer?
- What should visitors do on the website?
- How many pages or products are needed?
- Who will provide text, images, logo, and product data?
- Does the client need to edit content themselves?
- Are payments, bookings, accounts, or integrations required?
- What is the desired launch date and budget range?

### Delivery workflow

1. Introductory call or message.
2. Confirm the appropriate package.
3. Send a one-page proposal and quotation.
4. Collect deposit and content.
5. Confirm sitemap and visual direction.
6. Build the website.
7. Conduct client review and apply included revisions.
8. Test on common screen sizes and browsers.
9. Collect final payment.
10. Launch, hand over access, and request a testimonial/referral.

## 8. First 90-day launch plan

### Weeks 1–2: Foundation

- Choose a business name and service positioning.
- Define the visual identity and proposal template.
- Build the business landing page.
- Prepare one basic website demo and one admin-portal demo.

### Weeks 3–4: Proof

- Publish package prices and scope.
- Create screenshots and short demo videos.
- Offer the first 2–3 clients a controlled launch discount in exchange for permission to use the work as case studies.
- Document delivery time and actual hours spent.

### Month 2: Outreach

- Contact local businesses, friends, professional networks, and startup communities.
- Post useful examples on LinkedIn, Facebook, Instagram, or TikTok.
- Build referral partnerships with designers, photographers, marketers, and accountants.
- Ask every satisfied client for one testimonial and two introductions.

### Month 3: Improve margins

- Review which features caused scope creep.
- Convert repeated requests into priced add-ons.
- Raise prices for new clients if delivery is consistently full.
- Create a clear one-off support and update quotation process.
- Focus marketing on the most profitable customer segment.

## 9. Pricing rules for protecting profit

- Sell outcomes and scope, not unlimited time.
- Never promise “unlimited revisions.”
- Do not begin development before the deposit and required content are received.
- Keep the custom package flexible, but always write a defined scope for each project.
- Add a contingency of approximately 15–25% to custom estimates for uncertainty.
- Track actual hours for every project.
- If a package repeatedly takes longer than planned, either reduce scope or increase price.
- Treat urgent delivery as a premium service, for example an additional 25–50%.
